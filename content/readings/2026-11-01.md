## Introducing the Lesson
In Colossians 3 and 4, Paul challenges believers to embrace higher ideals and values because they have left behind their former way of life. Instead of living to please the world, they are now living to please the King of kings.

## Commentary and Application

### 1. Live a New Life
#### a. Think about Things Above – Colossians 3:1–4

New life starts the moment we decide to follow Jesus. His death cancels our sins, and His resurrection ensures that we will rise again one day. Being unified with Jesus elevates us to a spiritual standing we had not held before: "raised to new life with Christ" who "sits in the place of honour at God's right hand" (Colossians 3:1). Because of all this, we should "set [our] sights on the realities of heaven." What are these realities or "things above" (NIV)? They are summed up in Philippians 4:8, where Paul writes, "Fix your thoughts on what is true, and honourable, and right, and pure, and lovely, and admirable. Think about things that are excellent and worthy of praise."

When Jesus gives us new life, we also gain a new perspective on what really matters. Our priorities and values start to align with heavenly things, not earthly things (Colossians 3:2). Instead of loving the world, we become committed to Jesus. Our lives are hidden in Him (verse 3) in the sense that our bond with Him is spiritual and invisible. But since we are being completely transformed from the inside out, the change in our attitudes and actions should be visible to the people around us.

One day, when Jesus returns in glory, everything hidden will be revealed. There will be no question who Jesus is. There will be no question who belongs to Him. If we are His, we "will share in all his glory" (verse 4). Once and for all, the forces of evil will be defeated, and King Jesus will reign unopposed.

##### Questions for Application
- How does setting your heart on heavenly things affect your thinking and priorities?

- How can we stay ready for Jesus' return?

#### b. Leave Your Sins Behind – Colossians 3:5–7

The sins listed in Colossians 3:5 likely describe the Colossians' way of life before they began following Jesus (verse 7). Living in an idolatrous society where these sins were prevalent, some of the Colossians may have been tempted to return to the sins from which they had been delivered. To prevent this from happening, Paul commanded them to "put to death the sinful, earthly things lurking within you" (verse 5). He gave similar instruction to the Roman believers: "Consider yourselves to be dead to the power of sin and alive to God through Christ Jesus" (Romans 6:11). Jesus' followers have been set apart for God, so they should live accordingly.

Note how the sins that Paul describes in Colossians 3:5 begin in the heart (lust and greed) and then are manifested physically (sexual immorality and idol worship). While the human body is not inherently evil, it can become the instrument by which sin is carried out. Our physical bodies behave according to whatever spiritual principles govern our hearts (Matthew 15:19).

Because of this ongoing sin, God's anger will one day be poured out on those who have rejected Him (Colossians 3:6). God loves sinners and wants them to be reconciled to Him, but if wicked people continue to refuse His grace, Scripture is clear that they will face His judgement.

When was the last time you thanked God for forgiving your sin, adopting you into His family, and giving you new life through Jesus? As God continually works in you, changing you from the inside out, Colossians 3:7 can be part of your testimony: "You used to do these things when your life was still part of this world." Like Paul, we can never claim we've achieved perfection on this side of eternity (Philippians 3:12). But we can remember where God has brought us from and "press on to reach the end of the race and receive the heavenly prize for which God, through Christ Jesus, is calling us" (verse 14).

##### Questions for Application
- Why must we continually put to death our sinful desires?

- How would you summarise your before-and-after testimony of following Jesus?

### 2. Put on Your New Nature
#### a. Jesus Lives in Us – Colossians 3:8–11

In Colossians 3:8–9, Paul listed sins that believers should eradicate from their lives, including angry attitudes and outbursts, hateful behaviour, and words that are damaging, obscene, or dishonest. When reading lists of vices such as these, believers sometimes rank them based on which ones seem more or less serious. For example, Paul lists murder and gossip together in Romans 1:29. Most believers would admit to viewing one of those sins as far more severe than the other. However, all sin disrupts our connection with God, bringing harm to others and ourselves. That's why Paul told the Colossians it was time to remove their "old sinful nature and all its wicked deeds" (3:9).

Once our sinful nature has been laid aside, we are to put on our new nature. This is an ongoing process of renewal as we follow Jesus—learning who God is and beginning to reflect His nature more and more (verse 10). The things that used to divide us—like religious or ethnic background—no longer define us. Instead, "Christ is all that matters, and he lives in all of us" (verse 11).

##### Questions for Application

- Read Ephesians 4:21–24. What does this passage add to your understanding of what it means to put on your new nature?

- What is the difference between learning about God and getting to know God?

#### b. Clothed in Love – Colossians 3:12–14

As he often does in his letters, Paul follows up his list of vices with a list of virtues in Colossians 3:12–14. This is by no means a complete list of virtues, but they represent the goodness that should be evident in Christians' lives: heartfelt compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, patience, and the willingness to forgive. The New Testament clearly communicates that Christlike virtues should grow in the lives of believers, signifying new life and fruitfulness through the power of the Holy Spirit (Galatians 5:22–23).

Over everything else, Paul told the Colossians to "clothe [themselves] with love" (Colossians 3:14). Love is the supreme virtue unifying all believers. Jesus said the world would know we are His disciples because we love one another (John 13:35). This love is not emotional or conditional; it is the type of sacrificial love Jesus demonstrated on the cross. Even though we will not always like what people do, we can always show them love.

Jesus makes it possible for us to experience a new reality, to live a new life, to put on a new nature. Jesus "lives in all of us" (Colossians 3:11). God chose us, loves us, and has made us special (verse 12). These gifts from Him should motivate us to live in a way that pleases Him. He is the one who "began the good work within you" and He "will continue his work until it is finally finished on the day when Christ Jesus returns" (Philippians 1:6).

##### Questions for Application
- Why is love the supreme Christian virtue?

- Why do you think Paul so often used the imagery of "taking off" and "putting on" in his letters (Romans 13:12; 1 Corinthians 15:53; 2 Corinthians 5:2–4; Galatians 3:27; Ephesians 4:24; 6:11–17; Colossians 3:10–14)?

### 3. Imitate Jesus
#### a. Representing Jesus – Colossians 3:15–17

In Colossians 3:15, Paul continued his instructions on how to live as people who belong to Jesus and have put on a new nature. Along with letting sacrificial love be the governing principle of their lives, the Colossians were to let the peace of God rule in their hearts. How do we do that? We believe His promises, trust His goodness, and obey His Word. We cultivate unity with other believers since God has called us to live together in peaceful fellowship. Along with showing love and pursuing peace, we also exemplify gratitude.

The good news of Jesus should saturate every area of our lives and produce His character in us (verse 16). This kind of transformation requires a commitment to the lifelong endeavour of reading, studying, and living out God's Word. If we lack wisdom to know how to apply Scripture, we should "ask God, who gives generously to all without finding fault, and it will be given" to us (James 1:5, NIV). As we soak up God's Word, the Holy Spirit transforms us, enabling us to live fruitful lives for God. Paul explained that this fullness would overflow to others as we teach and counsel others and worship together with thankful hearts (Colossians 3:16).

We must remember that we represent Jesus in everything we do and say (verse 17). "God has given us this task of reconciling people to him... We are Christ's ambassadors; God is making his appeal through us. We speak for Christ when we plead, 'Come back to God!'" (2 Corinthians 5:18, 20).

##### Questions for Application
- What is the connection between letting the good news of Jesus fill our lives and being Jesus' ambassadors?

- Why do you think thankfulness is a theme in Colossians 3:15–17?

#### b. Proclaiming the Gospel – Colossians 4:2–6

Paul told the Colossians to devote themselves to prayer (Colossians 4:2). This kind of devotion carries the idea of strength, fervour, perseverance, and tenacity. According to Paul, it also requires alertness. When we pray, we should remain aware and ready to hear God speak to us. Unlike the believers in Acts 12:12–17 who were amazed when God answered their prayer, we should exercise faith, expecting God to respond.

Paul explained that prayer should be offered with thanksgiving. Even before God answers, we can thank Him for hearing us. What a privilege He has given us to be able to approach Him in prayer.

Paul asked the Colossians to pray that he would have opportunities to present the gospel with clarity—even from prison (Colossians 4:3–4). Preaching about Jesus was the reason Paul had been arrested in the first place, but he was still fully committed to the mission. Like the Colossians, we should also be missions-minded in our prayers. As Jesus told His disciples, "The harvest is great, but the workers are few. So pray to the Lord who is in charge of the harvest; ask him to send more workers into his fields" (Matthew 9:37–38). Even though some nations or regions of the world have shut their doors to the gospel, those barriers cannot stop the gospel. We should pray that political doors are opened and that people's hearts are opened, ready to receive the good news of Jesus. We should also pray along with Paul that we are able to present the gospel clearly and effectively. Unbelievers are blinded by sin. We need the power of the Holy Spirit to share the good news in a way they can understand and believe.

In Colossians 4:5, Paul told believers to use wisdom when interacting with unbelievers, always keeping the gospel in mind. Jesus reached out to those who needed forgiveness. We should follow His example. The way we talk to unbelievers and treat unbelievers will greatly influence their perception of the Church and Jesus himself (verse 6). Our speech should be gracious, not dismissive or offensive. Like properly seasoned food is appetising, speech that is seasoned with grace can make the gospel appetising to those who are lost and hungry for the truth.

##### Questions for Application
- How can you prepare yourself to "make the most of every opportunity" in your interactions with unbelievers (Colossians 4:5)?

- How do you think gracious words can prevent us from driving an unbeliever away from Jesus?

## Call to Discipleship
By confronting the false teaching that had infiltrated the church in Colossae, Paul taught believers—both then and now—how to live out their faith in Jesus. When we become Christians, we lay aside our old nature, leaving behind our sinful way of life. We put on our new nature, living to please God instead of ourselves. Our allegiance shifts from this temporary world to the eternal Kingdom. Instead of worshipping the things of this world, we now worship the King of kings.

## Ministry in Action
Memorise Colossians 3:1. Create a reminder (for your fridge, mirror, or home screen) with the verse on it to help you remember to keep your mind on the realities of heaven.

Ask God to help you do a spiritual wardrobe check. What do you need to take off? What do you need to put on?

Implement one idea for being more missions-minded, such as signing up for a missions prayer team or intentionally developing relationships with unbelievers.
