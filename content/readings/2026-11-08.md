## Introducing the Lesson

When used for one of the first four books of the New Testament, the word *Gospel* refers to the good-news story about the life, ministry, death, and resurrection of Jesus. John's Gospel was written toward the close of the first century AD, about 50–60 years after Jesus' ascension. As one of the twelve disciples, John was an eyewitness of the events he recorded (21:24–25). He'd had many years to reflect on the significance of Jesus' love, both for himself personally and for all people. 

The other three Gospels, written earlier, are similar enough that they are called the "Synoptic" (meaning "viewed together") Gospels. But John's years of careful remembrance and reflection led him, under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, to write the same good news in a new way. While the Gospel of John is in complete agreement with the fundamental truths and message of the other three, John emphasises facets of the story that contribute to a fuller understanding of the nature and work of Jesus.

## The Holy Scriptures: John 3:1–8, 12–18

> **1** There was a man of the Pharisees, named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews:

> **3** Jesus answered and said unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God.  
> **4** Nicodemus saith unto him, How can a man be born when he is old? can he enter the second time into his mother's womb, and be born?  
> **5** Jesus answered, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God.

> **7** Marvel not that I said unto thee, Ye must be born again.  
> **8** The wind bloweth where it listeth, and thou hearest the sound thereof, but canst not tell whence it cometh, and whither it goeth: so is every one that is born of the Spirit.

## Commentary and Application

### 1. New Birth by the Spirit

#### a. Born Again from Above – *John 3:1–4*

Nicodemus was an influential Pharisee, probably a member of the highest ruling council of the Jews called the Sanhedrin. He met with Jesus at night to discuss the things Jesus had begun to do and say. In the chapter before this, Jesus had turned water into wine at a wedding in Cana of Galilee (John 2:1–11). He had also driven the sellers and money changers from the temple in Jerusalem, engaging in a brief conversation about His authority to have done so (verses 12–25). Whatever Nicodemus had seen or heard, he felt compelled to speak with Jesus for himself. Visiting at night (3:2) may suggest some hesitancy about meeting in public view.

Nicodemus addressed Jesus as "Rabbi," a term of respect applied to teachers. He then expressed his belief that Jesus must have come from God. He called Jesus' activities "signs," which in John's Gospel is the distinctive word for Jesus' miracles that reveal His identity. Nicodemus affirmed that no one could do these things apart from God's presence. Jesus' reply in verse 3 confused him.

As the *New Living Translation* footnote indicates, the Greek phrase translated "born again" could also be rendered as "born from above" (that is, from God). The immediately following context (verses 4–8) shows Jesus' intended meaning. Jesus was referring to birth from God (verse 5, born of "the Spirit"), which is also a rebirth. Nicodemus misunderstood "born again" to have a biological meaning (verse 4).

This is one of several times in John's Gospel where wordplay or double meanings signal that Jesus' true meaning has been misunderstood. People respond differently when their understanding is challenged. Some reject Jesus. Others follow Him immediately. Nicodemus would have further questions, but Scripture seems to indicate he ultimately became a disciple. He would speak up among the Jewish religious leaders, somewhat defending Jesus (7:50–51). Later, he would join Joseph of Arimathea in financing Jesus' burial (19:38–40).

##### Questions for Application
- How do you think most people today define the phrase "born-again Christian"?
- How do you respond when you find Jesus' teachings difficult to understand?

#### b. Born of Water and the Spirit – *John 3:5–8*

Jesus' first response (John 3:5) to Nicodemus' question clarified the meaning of verse 3. The "again" of "born again" is really "from above," or in Jesus' words, "of water and the Spirit." Does "of water" refer to physical birth and "of the Spirit" to spiritual birth? Or are water and the Spirit both a part of the born-from-above spiritual birth? Though some consider water and Spirit to describe two different births, it is likely they describe the same second, "from above" birth (see Isaiah 44:3; Ezekiel 36:25–27; Joel 2:28). Titus 3:5 says, *"According to his mercy he saved us, by the washing of regeneration, and renewing of the Holy Ghost"* (KJV).

This spiritual birth is necessary for seeing (John 3:3) or entering (verse 5) the kingdom of God. Jesus goes on to contrast physical and spiritual birth (verse 6). The former produces physical life; the latter, spiritual life. As Jesus tells the Samaritan woman in 4:24, *"God is Spirit, so those who worship him must worship in spirit and in truth."* Only God's Holy Spirit can give birth to such worshippers, the kind of worshippers the Father seeks (verse 23). Earlier in his Gospel, John had introduced it this way: 

> *"To all who believed him [Jesus] and accepted him, he gave the right to become children of God. They are reborn—not with a physical birth resulting from human passion or plan, but a birth that comes from God"* (1:12–13).

Those born from above, God's children, will begin to take on the family likeness. As Peter says, God's promises *"enable you to share his divine nature"* (2 Peter 1:4).

After telling Nicodemus that he should not be surprised (John 3:7), Jesus acknowledged the mystery involved in new birth. His wind analogy was particularly apt since in both Greek and Hebrew the word for "spirit" can also be translated "wind." Wind exists though it cannot be seen; its effects are undeniable and cannot be dictated by people. Similarly, God's Holy Spirit is not seen but certainly acts as He wills (1 Corinthians 12:8–11).

##### Questions for Application
- What does it mean to enter the kingdom of God?
- What have been some of the effects of the Holy Spirit's regenerating work in your life since you were born again?
- Why is "new birth from above" such a fitting description of what God does for us?

### 2. Jesus Would Be Lifted Up

#### a. The Challenge of Believing – *John 3:9–12*

When Nicodemus asked, *"How can these things be?"* (John 3:9, KJV), he set the stage for an extended speech that includes the most well-known verse in the Bible.

Jesus' own question—*"You don't understand these things?"* (verse 10)—highlights again that Nicodemus, a master teacher among his people, should have readily recognised, believed, and received this truth.

In verse 11, Jesus' use of "we" instead of "I" points to His unity with the Father. "We" implies what will be made clear later in this Gospel: Jesus speaks His Father's words (3:34; 14:10; 17:8). His address to "you" (plural in Greek) includes, along with Nicodemus, all who struggle with believing. Some ultimately come to faith; others will continue in unbelief and rejection.

Jesus returns in 3:12 to the earthly/heavenly division first mentioned in verse 6. Nicodemus, "born of the flesh" (verse 6, KJV) at this point, has not even believed in Jesus' earthly teachings, perhaps referring to His wind analogy. If even at this lower level Nicodemus is resistant, how can he be receptive to the heavenly things that only Jesus can teach him? Nicodemus himself proves that eternal life requires being born from above, "born of the Spirit" (KJV).

It is not enough to merely respect Jesus as a great teacher, acknowledge His miraculous works, or even admit that God is with Him (verse 2). This is the challenge of believing: Jesus' testimony must be believed in full. Jesus not only speaks truth from the Father; Jesus *is* the truth spoken by the Father. Hebrews 1 contrasts God's former communication through Israel's prophets with the ultimate revelation: *"In these last days he has spoken to us by his Son"* (Hebrews 1:2, NIV). He is *"the way and the truth and the life"* (John 14:6, NIV) and the sole *"stairway between heaven and earth"* (1:51). There is no other path to the Father and eternal life. Hence John's purpose in writing his Gospel: *"that you may believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name"* (20:31, NIV).

##### Questions for Application
- Though Jesus is the only way to God, what other paths do people try?
- Do you know anyone who, like Nicodemus in John 3, is on the path toward believing in Jesus? How can you encourage them to fully believe the entire testimony about Him?

#### b. The Promise of Believing – *John 3:13–15*

As the Son of God and Son of Man (a title Jesus frequently used for himself), Jesus uniquely reveals God's nature. John 1:18 declares, *"No one has ever seen God. But the unique One, who is himself God, is near to the Father's heart. He has revealed God to us."* Similarly, 3:12–13 makes the point that Jesus alone has come down from heaven and can bear true witness to heavenly things.

In verse 14, Jesus refers to the account of Moses and the bronze serpent recorded in Numbers 21:4–9. Because the people had grumbled against God and Moses, the Lord sent venomous snakes among them, and many people died of snakebites. When the people repented, Moses prayed for them:

> *"Then the Lord told him, 'Make a replica of a poisonous snake and attach it to a pole. All who are bitten will live if they simply look at it!' So Moses made a snake out of bronze and attached it to a pole. Then anyone who was bitten by a snake could look at the bronze snake and be healed!"* (verses 8–9).

Jesus makes the comparison:

> *"As Moses lifted up the bronze snake on a pole in the wilderness, so the Son of Man must be lifted up, so that everyone who believes in him will have eternal life"* (John 3:14–15).

In the Old Testament, God provided a way for snakebitten Israelites to receive physical life by obeying God's instruction to look at a metal serpent lifted up on a pole. In the New Testament, God provided a way for all people to receive eternal life by putting their faith in the Son, who was lifted up by crucifixion. Amazing grace!

##### Question for Application
- What are some of the ways Jesus revealed God's nature?

### 3. Enter the Light

#### a. For God So Loved – *John 3:16–18*

After using the illustration of the bronze serpent to help Nicodemus grasp the significance of His mission, Jesus now moves to the source of the new life He has described. God's deep love for His created world leads to His generous grace. Doesn't all true love lead to sacrificial giving?

There could be no greater sacrifice than the one God gave: His one and only Son, Jesus (John 3:16). Jesus would be "lifted up" (verse 14) by crucifixion to receive the punishment required for the sins of the entire world. God has shown this love to all people, not just certain individuals, families, tribes, or nations. Every person is invited to receive eternal life by believing in and following Jesus.

The eternal life Jesus provides is about more than quantity (how long it lasts). It primarily refers to the quality of life that begins as soon as we surrender to Him. As Jesus prays in John 17:3, *"This is eternal life: that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom you have sent"* (NIV). It is the life of ultimate truth, of intimate relationship with the King of the ages. In verse 24, Jesus prays, *"Father, I want these whom you have given me to be with me where I am. Then they can see all the glory you gave me because you loved me even before the world began!"*

God's purpose in the giving of His Son was not condemnation but salvation (3:17). The gift is free, but the recipient must respond with faith. People are free to reject God's gift of salvation; He will not force it on anyone. Refusing to accept this gift of salvation through Jesus means a person is under God's judgement. In fact, people who will not believe in Jesus are *"condemned already"* (verse 18, NIV).

##### Questions for Application
- Why do you think John 3:16 is so well-known? How well do you think the average person understands its meaning?
- What aspect of eternal life in Jesus is the most significant to you?

#### b. From Darkness to Light – *John 3:19–21*

Because in the original Greek there are no quotation marks, it is difficult to determine at what point Jesus' conversation with Nicodemus ends and John's narration resumes. Either way, these verses are God's Word, whether spoken by Jesus or through John.

John 3:19 is among the saddest sentences in Scripture. At first it echoes John 1:1–5, describing Jesus as light and life, the Word shining in the darkness and offering so much hope and promise. But the second half is a scathing commentary on the way the world received Him. What madness, in a misguided attempt at self-preservation, to turn to the darkness instead of experiencing healing in the light.

C. S. Lewis' allegory *The Great Divorce* contains a story in which a man holds fearfully to his sin, which is portrayed as a lizard sitting on his shoulder and whispering lies in his ear. Persuaded finally to allow an angelic being to remove the lizard, the man fears he will die when the light touches him. Instead, the lizard transforms into a mighty stallion that serves the man instead of deceiving and disabling him. The man grows strong and noble in the light. If the people described in John 3:19–20 could just surrender their fear and pride, they would be gloriously set free by Jesus, the Light.

To provide a contrast, verse 21 describes a person who does the truth and comes to the light. The phrase "doeth truth" (KJV) is gripping. We often think of truth as something we can believe or know, but *doing* truth is even more robust. In fact, it is living proof that we have moved from darkness into light. As John writes in a later letter:

> *"This is the message we heard from Jesus and now declare to you: God is light, and there is no darkness in him at all … If we are living in the light, as God is in the light, then we have fellowship with each other, and the blood of Jesus, his Son, cleanses us from all sin."*  
> — **1 John 1:5, 7**

##### Questions for Application
- Why is it so difficult to bring our sin into God's light? How can we overcome these hurdles?
- What does it look like to not just know or believe the truth, but to do the truth?

## Call to Discipleship

As people who have been born again through God's Spirit, we need to bear the family likeness, doing the truth as God empowers and enables us. As Paul says in Galatians 5:25, we need to *"keep in step with the Spirit"* (NIV), exhibiting the Spirit's fruit and exercising the Spirit's gifts. Let us step into the wind of the Holy Spirit, letting Him guide us where He desires, doing the truth and living in the light of God. Let us seek His guidance in prayer every day so we can walk in His truth and light in every aspect of our lives.
